## 1. 🧩 The LEGO Assembly Line

**Question**

What type of bond links amino acids together to form a protein? Which amino acids form hydrogen bonds with each other?

**Answer**

Amino acids are linked together by peptide bonds, formed between the carboxyl group (-COOH) of one amino acid and the amino group (-NH₂) of another. The backbone carbonyl oxygen (C=O) and amide N–H groups can form hydrogen bonds. Arginine (Arg) and lysine (Lys) can also participate in hydrogen bonding through their side chains.

## 2. 🧬 The Double-Track Highway of Life’s Blueprint

**Question**

How do nucleotides assemble to form double-stranded DNA? What are the basic structural components of DNA? Is DNA right-handed or left-handed? List the names of the four DNA bases. Which bases form hydrogen bonds with each other?

**Answer**

Nucleotides are connected by phosphodiester bonds to form the sugar-phosphate backbone of DNA. Each nucleotide consists of deoxyribose, a phosphate group, and a nitrogenous base. The two DNA strands form a double helix and are usually right-handed in the common B-DNA form. The four DNA bases are adenine (A), thymine (T), guanine (G), and cytosine (C). Adenine pairs with thymine through two hydrogen bonds, while guanine pairs with cytosine through three hydrogen bonds.

## 4. 🔎 Return to the Crime Scene

**Question**

Why can a mutation (change) in just one amino acid of a protein sometimes lead to a serious human disease? Give one specific example of such a disease.

**Answer**

A single amino acid mutation can alter a protein's three-dimensional structure, stability, or interactions with other molecules, thereby impairing its normal function. For example, mutations in TP53 can impair p53-mediated tumor suppression and contribute to the development of cancer.
